About

Fifteen years running operations taught me how people actually change — it wasn't in a boardroom.

I spent the first half of my career running operations teams at fast-growing companies — hiring, restructuring, hitting the number, doing it again. I was good at it. I was also, for most of it, quietly burning out, and I didn't have language for that until I hit a wall hard enough to stop.

Rebuilding after that wall taught me more about how people actually change than any leadership seminar did.

I trained as a coach through the International Coaching Federation and have spent the years since working with people at the same kind of wall — a role that no longer fits, a decision deferred so long it's calcified, a life that looks fine on paper and doesn't feel like theirs. My approach borrows more from operations than from therapy: we get specific, we build a plan, and we check whether it worked.

The approach

Three stages, in order, every time. It's a method, not a mood.

01

Reflect

We separate what's actually happening from the story you've been telling yourself about it. Most people arrive somewhere between the two, and it's rarely obvious which is which.

02

Reframe

Once the facts are on the table, we test the assumptions underneath them — the ones about what you're capable of, what you owe other people, and what "too late" actually means.

03

Rise

We build a plan with a first step small enough to actually take this week, and a way to check in on it — because insight without follow-through is just an expensive conversation.

A single 90-minute intensive for a specific decision you're stuck on — a job offer, a hard conversation, a move. You leave with clarity and a next step, not a syllabus.

Most common

Format12 weeks, biweekly

Small group

The Cohort

Six weeks with a small group of six, all working through a career or life transition at the same time. Structured sessions plus the accountability of doing it alongside others.

Format6 weeks, groups of 6
